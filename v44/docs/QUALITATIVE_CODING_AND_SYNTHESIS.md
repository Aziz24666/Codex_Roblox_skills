# Qualitative coding and synthesis

## Use a stable rubric

Define a small topic taxonomy (onboarding, core loop, progression, interface, controls, performance, social, accessibility, monetization, other), experience stage, source, sentiment, and impact. Write definitions for reviewers and allow mixed/neutral classifications.

Do not interpret a code as a diagnosis. “Confusing” is a participant perception; it does not prove the cause. Separate a report of a defect from a preference or a request.

## Inter-reviewer quality

Double-code ambiguous and high-impact submissions. Record disagreement, reconcile with the rubric, and note changes to definitions. Do not silently remove a contradictory submission or force agreement. Keep verbatim source material out of the V44 dataset.

## Summaries

V44 reports descriptive counts of coded submissions and suppresses groups below the predeclared small-cell threshold. It does not calculate population percentages, sentiment scores, causal effects, or statistical significance. A count is not a count of unique players unless the study process independently verifies that property.

The summary exposes a broad size band rather than an exact total and omits the number of suppressed categories to reduce the chance of reconstructing small groups through subtraction.

Describe who was invited, how they were recruited, what was not observed, and which devices/builds/languages were absent. Clearly label convenience and support-derived samples as non-representative.

## From theme to test

For each high-impact theme, verify the authorized source evidence, check reproducibility, identify alternative explanations, and design a separate QA task or experiment if needed. Do not treat popularity or comment frequency as a release requirement.
