# Roblox player feedback study design

## Define the decision before the questions
- Name the product or gameplay decision the study should inform.
- State what is currently unknown and what evidence would change the next step.
- Limit each study to one main question and a small number of supporting questions.
- Identify the build, environment, collection window, method, owner, and intended audience.
- Separate usability observation, preference feedback, bug reports, and product requests.

## Choose a fit-for-purpose method
- Use moderated playtests to observe task completion, confusion, and unexpected behavior.
- Use structured surveys for narrowly scoped perceptions after a relevant experience.
- Use support summaries to identify recurring issue categories, not to estimate prevalence.
- Avoid leading prompts, forced-positive language, and questions that ask for unnecessary personal details.
- Explain the study purpose, voluntary nature, skip option, and how submitted feedback will be used.

## Plan sampling honestly
- Define the eligible audience and recruitment channel before collection.
- Record likely self-selection, device, experience, language, and build limitations at an aggregate level only when useful.
- Set a realistic response target and a small-cell reporting threshold.
- Do not describe a convenience sample as representative of all players.
- Do not compare tiny cohorts or publish a category that could reveal an individual.

## Prepare before inviting participants
- Verify the current build and test environment.
- Prepare neutral tasks and prompts with observable success criteria.
- Decide how bugs, player-safety reports, and sensitive disclosures are routed to trained human reviewers.
- Set a retention period and a responsible owner for deletion.
- Keep research notes separate from account, moderation, support, and production credentials.

## Deliverable
Produce a study brief with the research question, audience, method, prompt set, notice, collection limits, sampling caveats, retention owner, and the specific decision that the evidence can inform.
