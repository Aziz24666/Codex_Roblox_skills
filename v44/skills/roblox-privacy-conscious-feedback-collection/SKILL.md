# Privacy-conscious Roblox feedback collection

## Tell participants what is happening
- Explain the study purpose, what categories will be recorded, who can review them, and the planned retention period.
- Make participation voluntary and allow participants to skip questions or stop.
- Do not imply that participation changes rewards, moderation outcomes, access, or account standing.
- Use age-appropriate, platform-compliant notices and obtain any required approvals before collection.

## Collect the minimum useful evidence
- Prefer structured categories and observed task outcomes over unrestricted text boxes.
- Do not collect usernames, account IDs, email addresses, contact details, IP addresses, device fingerprints, precise location, credentials, or unrelated personal information.
- Do not ask participants to share personal stories, screenshots, chat logs, or identifying details.
- Do not use a persistent cross-study identifier to join feedback to gameplay history.
- Keep operational diagnostics and research feedback in separate systems with separate access controls.

## Handle unexpected disclosures safely
- Do not copy sensitive disclosures into a research spreadsheet or analytics event.
- Route urgent safety concerns and policy-sensitive reports to the established human safety/moderation process.
- Never ask a researcher or automated script to determine whether a disclosure is true.
- Preserve only the minimum approved case reference needed for an authorized handoff.

## Secure, retain, and delete
- Restrict raw research material to the smallest approved group.
- Store only opaque, study-scoped references in aggregate work products.
- Set a short, documented retention period and verify deletion at the end.
- Keep API keys, admin credentials, moderation data, and player identifiers out of the research repository.
- Treat a tool's privacy checklist as a declaration to verify, not proof of lawful or platform-compliant collection.
